# A pen tool for Google Slides™

Install SVG to Slides. With nothing selected, press Option+Shift+P (Alt+Shift+P on Windows) or click Pen on the toolbar. Click to place corners, drag for curves, click the first point to close the shape, then click Done. Each path becomes a native Slides shape.

_Free forever. Every feature, no daily cap, no account, no Pro tier._

- Install the Chrome extension: https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/svg-to-slides/bdhhnakellmdnbgbdokiojipaebdeioi
- See every editing tool: https://svgslid.es/vector-editor

## How to do it
1. **Open the pen.** With nothing selected, press Option+Shift+P or click Pen on the toolbar. An empty editor opens over the slide.
2. **Place points.** Click for a corner point. To make a curve, press, drag and release.
3. **Close the shape.** Click the first point. A ring appears on it when the pointer is over it. To leave the path open, press Enter or Esc.
4. **Click Done.** The new shapes are placed on the slide, on top, in one undo step.

## Pen keys
- Sharp turn after a curve: hold Option while dragging to break the handles.
- Keep to 45° steps: hold Shift.
- Remove the last point: Delete.
- Carry on an open path: click its end point.
- Add a point to a path: click a segment. Click a point to remove it.
- Switch to the Select tool: V.
All pen keys · Snapping

## What you get
- Each path becomes a new shape. The first shape you draw has a 2 pixel dark stroke and no fill. Each later shape starts with the paint of the last one drawn.
- Set a fill or stroke from the editor’s toolbar before you click Done, or afterwards on the slide.
- With shapes selected when you open the pen, the editor opens on those shapes, and the pen can edit them and draw new ones beside them.

## Questions

### Is there a freehand mode?
No. The pen draws point by point.

### Can I reshape a drawn shape later?
Yes. Double-click it and click Edit Shape to move its points and curves. How to edit points.

### Can I leave a path open, like a line?
Yes. Press Enter or Esc instead of clicking the first point.
